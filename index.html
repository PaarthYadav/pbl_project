Medical Insurance Cost Prediction Analysis Using Machine Learning

"Can machine learning algorithms accurately predict medical insurance costs based on demographic and health-related factors to enable personalized insurance pricing and better financial planning?"

Problem Statement

The escalating costs and complexities in the healthcare sector underscore the necessity for efficient predictive models to anticipate medical insurance prices. Rising healthcare costs make it difficult for individuals to predict future medical expenses, while insurance providers struggle to offer personalized plans without data-driven insights. Traditional actuarial methods often encounter difficulties in capturing the intricate interrelations and dynamic nature of various factors affecting insurance pricing.

Literature Review

Analyzed digital health startups transforming healthcare, AI-powered diagnostics and predictive analytics, existing regression-based insurance prediction models, and SHAP-based feature importance analysis techniques.

Research Gap & Innovation

Lack of personalized predictions for individual patients, limited use of advanced ensemble techniques (XGBoost, Gradient Boosting), and challenges in accessing comprehensive datasets. Our approach compares 9 regression models to identify the optimal predictor.

Key Findings

Smoking Impact

Smoking status is the most significant predictor of insurance costs, with smokers paying substantially higher premiums than non-smokers.

BMI Correlation

Higher BMI values correlate with increased insurance charges, especially when combined with smoking behavior.

Age Factor

Insurance costs increase progressively with age, reflecting higher healthcare utilization among older individuals.

Ensemble Methods Excel

XGBoost and Gradient Boosting significantly outperformed traditional regression methods, achieving 86%+ accuracy.
