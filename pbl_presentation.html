MACHINE LEARNING-BASED CLASSIFICATION OF CARDIAC ARRHYTHMIA: A MULTI-ALGORITHM APPROACH

"Can classical machine learning algorithms, coupled with PCA dimensionality reduction on ECG data, accurately detect and classify cardiac arrhythmia into 16 distinct groups for automated early medical diagnosis?"

Problem Statement

Electrocardiogram (ECG) readouts are essential diagnostic tools for cardiac health, but manual interpretation over large-scale hospital datasets is highly time-consuming and error-prone. A single irregular heartbeat could be harmless, but persistent arrhythmias may cause heart failure or stroke. This project seeks to confront the challenges of high clinical dimensionality (279 features per patient) and severely imbalanced class distributions to automate reliable early arrhythmia classification.

Literature Review

Explored existing cardiology analysis standards (Guvenir et al., 1997) utilizing the UCI ML Repository, reviewed advanced preprocessing techniques to counteract multilinearity through Principal Component Analysis (PCA), and studied synthetic oversampling mechanics (SMOTE) to rebalance clinical data.

Research Gap & Innovation

Real-world ECG data is sparsely distributed, often leaving vital minority defect types with barely 2–5 diagnostic samples. Our system tackles this "Curse of Dimensionality" by mapping data effectively down to 95% variance limits and implementing 6 predictive classifiers optimized strictly for maximal Recall rates.

Key Findings

Combating Dimensionality

Attempting to map 279 diverse features across just 452 patient sets naturally collapses accuracy. Feature-extraction using PCA mitigated complex multicollinear ECG properties, securing model convergence.

Class Equilibrium

Since 'Normal' heartbeats comprised 54% of all cases—and minority conditions existed as rarely as dual measurements—Synthetic Minority Oversampling (SMOTE) proved absolutely critical for preventing skewed decision boundaries.

Linear Separability Limits

The complex overlap across diverse cardiac faults demanded non-linear strategies. The kernel-trick equipped Support Vector Machine accurately grouped features previously blended together in initial datasets.

Targeting High Recall

Algorithmic selection solely for accuracy proves functionally risky within cardiological domains. Designating Recall as the chief parameter inherently shields downstream patient prognosis against deceptive 'False Negative' results.
